# Dalaal Street

Dalaal Street is a browser-based market game for synchronized events and classroom activities. Players trade a fictional Indian stock market while a host controls the shared session. It is a learning simulation: prices and company news are generated by the game and are not live market data or investment advice.

## How the game works

- Each participant gets a device session and starts with ₹10,00,000 of simulated cash.
- There are exactly 10 live terminals. Enter the assigned two-digit terminal code at `/play`, or open its direct URL from `/play/01` to `/play/10`; each code maps to that terminal's player session.
- Players can buy shares, sell shares they own, or open short positions. A **Sell all** control liquidates every open long holding in one confirmed action; short positions remain open. Prices move as the market simulation, news events, and participant trades update the shared market.
- A host can start, pause, and end a timed batch; set the next batch to 4–15 minutes; adjust market behavior; publish or schedule bulletins; and review participant sessions.
- The curated news library has 61 fictional bulletins: 12 economy stories, 3 for each of the 8 sectors, and 25 company stories for 25 different stocks. The remaining stocks still respond to their sector and economy stories. Hosts can select any mix of categories in Quick Access or News Studio, review the combined stock-by-stock estimate, and expand a headline for its individual effect before publishing.
- A new batch reset restores the market's starting stock prices as well as clearing its terminal sessions and published headlines.
- Host presence uses each device's last-seen heartbeat. A new batch resets participant portfolios without marking previously offline terminals as connected.
- Price movement is driven by 128 simulated traders with 16 distinct trading styles. The baseline volatility is 15% lower than the previous engine setting; hosts can still adjust volatility for an event.
- The audience wall shows the Dalaal-10 level and change, all stock prices and changes, only the latest published news set, and active terminal cash, P&L and total portfolio value without creating a participant account.
- Audience screens fit the full stock board and latest news into one viewport; the stock grid changes from 7×4 on wide screens to denser responsive layouts on smaller screens. There are no page or news scrollbars on the audience wall.
- Trading screens show a ticker of all stock prices above the market. Only the latest published news set appears near the top of the trading page in a full news panel with numbered stories, scope, major/minor labels, game-relative time and an internal close button. Older sets remain available in the News desk. The live stock ticker runs 15% slower.
- The game includes an isolated practice market with the same three-second countdown, six-minute rounds, trading rules, news flow, settlement, and session report as a hosted game.
- Each player’s round report includes short, activity-based learning notes, badges tied to their choices, and their best and toughest realized closed trades.
- Host notes are stored with the shared game state and are available after sign-in from another device.

## Latest host and display refinements

- Overview shows the live batch clock, start/pause/end controls, terminal status and equity, recent headlines, and shortcuts to frequently used sections. Open it with **Alt + O**.
- Quick Access and News Studio share a faster news desk: category tabs, search, sector and direction filters, a removable selection list, publish/schedule controls, target effects, a reaction timer, and collapsible automation settings. Published picks are cleared automatically.
- Simultaneous news uses the most specific applicable scope for each stock: company news overrides sector news for that company, and sector news overrides economy news within that sector. Stories at the same scope combine.
- News prices no longer jump on publication. The direct price move builds over 35 seconds with small, smooth retracements before reaching the same target. The follow-through eases in instead of front-loading the opening seconds. Its integrated impact is conserved, while the complete effect takes about 15% longer than before (typically 61–94 seconds, within the displayed 58–104 second range). Unrelated background noise is softened only during the direct reaction, so small announcements remain readable while still allowing retracements. Market trading continues alongside news, so displayed effects are estimates, not guaranteed final prices. Pausing freezes news progress; the host timer includes the remaining reaction and follow-through.
- Autonomous news is available at `/auto`. Anyone can start the next shared batch there; that round begins with the news brain enabled. Once started, it selects, reviews, reschedules, and publishes compatible sets of two or three unused stories on its own, without requiring host approval. It studies Dalaal-10 movement, recent price volatility, active terminals, trading activity, and outstanding news reactions, and reviews its choices every five simulated seconds. Repeated reviews keep the original time anchor so publication cannot be postponed indefinitely. The default interval is 75 seconds; the host can set a 50–90 second baseline, and the brain can adapt the next release between 50 and 105 seconds. The host can still monitor and override it.
- The dedicated News brain tab (**Alt + B**) holds the detailed readings, plan, pacing and activity log. Quick Access and News Studio show only a compact start/stop row and a shortcut into that tab. The host can start or stop the brain during an existing batch, review its live market readings, see the upcoming stories and reason, skip and reselect, publish early, replace the next set with two or three host-selected stories, and review the batch activity log. This remains available even when someone else starts the round through `/auto`. Manual news selection remains available when the brain is stopped.
- Audience company names are one pixel larger, and trade input values use theme-aware text and field colors in both light and dark mode.
- The browser tab uses the Dalaal Street brand mark as its favicon.

## Run locally

Requirements: Node.js 20.9 or newer and pnpm.

```sh
pnpm install
cp .env.example .env.local
pnpm dev
```

Open `http://localhost:3000`. Configure the environment values before using the shared market:

- `SUPABASE_URL`: Supabase project URL.
- `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`: Supabase publishable (anonymous) API key.
- `MARKET_SIGNING_SECRET`: a long random secret used by the server to authorize market-state RPC calls.
- `HOST_PASSWORD`: password for `/host`.

`/play` asks for the station's terminal code; `/play/01` through `/play/10` open the assigned numbered player sessions directly. `/v1` is the original look, V2 is an observatory, V3 uses claymorphism, V4 uses expressive flowing forms, V5 is a restrained signal board, V6 is a record store, and V7 uses cut-paper geometry. Every version uses the same market engine, company data, and trade rules. Trading screens adapt their stock columns to screen size and browser zoom; stocks stay on the left, with the selected stock's chart and Buy, Sell, and Short controls together on the right, directly below the chart. Chart markers and hover details show trades from the current terminal's individual session. Audience versions show the Dalaal-10 level and change, the full stock list with each price and change, latest batch headlines, and terminal position summaries.

Starting stock prices use rounded whole-rupee reference closes from NSE India's market page dated 15 October 2026 (15:30 IST); Wipro's close for the same date uses Yahoo Finance historical data. These are opening references for a fictional game and are not live quotes. The initial daily move percentages are preserved to reflect that dated market snapshot.

The practice market uses the same price and curated news simulation as a live round, but has its own state. It starts with a short countdown, runs for six minutes, closes positions at the end, and offers a new practice round. `/test/01` to `/test/10` use their own terminal logins. Legacy autonomous practice sessions are closed safely the first time the updated engine reads them.

During local development, open `http://localhost:3000/ui-preview` to compare the original interface and all six themed versions. Preview market state stays in its own in-memory development store and cannot affect the shared live market. Preview routes return not found in production.

## Supabase setup

Apply `supabase/migrations/20261009000100_market_state.sql` to the project using the Supabase SQL Editor or Supabase CLI. The migration creates the private JSONB market-state table and its access-controlled RPC functions.

Add a row to `private.runtime_secrets` with the same value as `MARKET_SIGNING_SECRET`:

```sql
insert into private.runtime_secrets (id, market_signing_secret)
values (true, 'replace-with-the-same-random-secret')
on conflict (id) do update
set market_signing_secret = excluded.market_signing_secret;
```

Keep the signing secret and host password out of source control. The Supabase publishable key is designed to be public, but this app still keeps it server-side. For Vercel, configure `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, `MARKET_SIGNING_SECRET`, and `HOST_PASSWORD` as environment variables, then redeploy. The host-console login reads `HOST_PASSWORD` server-side; update it in Vercel's environment settings to reset that password. `vercel.json` selects the Mumbai (`bom1`) region.

## Project layout

- `app/components/trader.tsx` — participant market, portfolio, news desk, charts, and audience wall.
- `app/components/host-console.tsx` — host controls, news selection, and participant session view.
- `src/game/catalog.ts` — fictional companies, starting prices, and the direction-checked economy, sector and company news catalog.
- `src/game/engine.ts` — market ticks, trades, news effects, account valuation, autonomous news controls, and session settlement.
- `src/game/autonomous-news.ts` — market and trading signals, adaptive news timing, and two- or three-story selection.
- `src/game/store.ts` — shared Supabase market state, plus a separate in-memory store for local UI previews.
- `src/game/types.ts` — shared market and participant types.
- `src/ui-options.ts`, `app/ui-theme-v*.css`, and `app/ui-preview/` — version descriptions, separate visual theme styles, and local preview pages.
- `supabase/migrations/` — database objects required by the shared game state.

The market state is stored as JSONB and shared across app instances through Supabase RPC calls. The game does not use real-time exchange prices.

## Network behavior

The market keeps its one-second update rhythm and price rules unchanged. Unchanged snapshots return a small `304` response, charts send only new candles after their initial history load, inactive screens stop requesting chart candles, and host snapshots omit chart history. All 28 company marks load from small local SVG assets, with a sector glyph fallback if an asset cannot load; screens use system fonts. Disconnected or hidden terminals stop polling; transient request failures back off automatically before retrying.

## News and standings refinements

- The library still contains 61 stories: 12 economy, three for each of eight sectors, and 25 company stories. Each now describes a concrete event and a clear business consequence in everyday language. Stories have event topics so opposite accounts of the same event cannot be selected together or later in the same batch. The brain also reserves manually scheduled stories.
- Audience and trading news use individual rounded cards showing the full details of only the latest published set, including headings, counts, category/scope labels and game-relative release times. Audience news stays inside one viewport without scrolling. Its close button sits inside the section. A new set appears automatically.
- The trading account bar shows the terminal’s position by total portfolio value. Results show the final position when the batch has closed; an individually finished session shows its position so far while other terminals continue trading. Ranking counts one current login per included terminal, including finished and offline entrants. Equal portfolio values share a rank.
- Browser checks cover all seven versions in light and dark themes at desktop, laptop, tablet and mobile widths. Engine verification: `node scripts/engine-smoke.cjs` and `node scripts/news-behavior-check.cjs`.

## Compact brain and situation-aware pacing

- The opening set uses two clear company stories in different sectors, with no more than one major story. The second introduces a sector story alongside a company in another sector. Later sets may use broader combinations.
- Decisions consider session duration, elapsed and remaining time, recent buys/sells/shorts, engaged terminals, positions, unused cash, aggregate portfolio stress and market trends. These are game signals, not a reliable diagnosis of player understanding.
- Quick Access and News Studio keep a compact start/stop row. The dedicated News brain tab keeps its state, Start/Stop, the upcoming stories, release timing and a short decision reason visible. Detailed readings, settings, decision rules, activity and pacing controls are grouped under Advanced. Buttons use their content width so descriptions do not get squeezed into one word per line.
- The brain uses explicit decision rules, calibrated with simulated games. It can choose a calmer two-story set when parents need reading time, a livelier set of fresh opportunities when the market is quiet, or a balanced set when trading is steady. It measures engagement across terminals, recent moves relative to the configured volatility, outstanding effects, held/traded companies and recent topic coverage. It does not use a player’s wealth to favour their results.
- Follow-up stories about the same company or sector are allowed when relevant; repeated headlines, contradictory accounts of the same event and stories reserved by the host are excluded. Normally prior news gets time to settle. A short tail may overlap when the market is quiet, and the brain avoids starting an unfinished new reaction in the closing seconds. The host can still publish a set early.
- The brain may adjust only volatility and bot activity. It observes for 45 seconds, changes at most 5% of the host baseline every 30 seconds, and stays between 75% and 110% of that baseline (within existing setting limits). Disabled bots stay disabled. Every adjustment appears in host activity and expanded readings. Host edits become the new baseline; stopping/resetting/ending restores that baseline. Cash, duration, tick speed, market bias and trading rules are not changed.
- Live price/chart update cadence is unchanged. The scrolling stock-price strip remains 15% slower than its original speed.
- Regression and calibration commands: `node scripts/engine-smoke.cjs`, `node scripts/news-behavior-check.cjs`, `node scripts/brain-scenarios.cjs`, `node scripts/news-direction-check.cjs`, `node scripts/brain-settings-check.cjs`. Scenario tests cover four-, six- and fifteen-minute rounds with spectators, cautious players, active players and a busy market. Direction tests check all 61 stories at three volatility settings: the initial move against publication price, later news contribution against an identically moderated neutral control, and exact isolation of unaffected stocks.

## Shared host logs

- Open **Logs** from the host sidebar or press **Alt + G**. Filter by activity or batch, search for a company/terminal/action, expand event data, load older entries, or download matching records as JSON.
- Records include batch controls, host settings and interventions, terminal joins/reconnections, successful trades and covers, rejected orders, news sets and settling, brain plans and decisions, automatic difficulty adjustments, final settlements, and periodic market checkpoints. Routine price ticks and note contents are excluded. Logging starts with this update; earlier activity cannot be reconstructed.
- The latest 1,500 events persist in the existing shared game store across batches and devices. Reset retains this journal. Player/audience responses exclude logs entirely. Host polling requests journal data only while Logs is open and fetches new entries after the last received event, avoiding repeated downloads of the full history.
- The brain combines current market/participant data with recent logged order difficulties. Repeated failures across active terminals encourage calmer news and gentler market settings; they never cause portfolio changes or changes to trading rules.
- Additional regression: `node scripts/game-logs-check.cjs` checks the actual API handlers, rejected-order persistence without portfolio changes, trade retry deduplication, news and settlement records, privacy, retention and decision signals.

### Host brain details

### News direction in brain-led games and updated identity

- The mark is now a compact vector D with a market line, paired with a shared “Dalaal Street” wordmark on terminals, audience screens, host access, terminal entry and the public launcher. The favicon uses the same mark.
- A new news set replaces unfinished effects on the companies it targets. Within that set, company news still takes priority over sector news, and sector news over economy news. Unaffected companies keep their current effects.
- Ordinary noise, bot pressure and old momentum are moderated for the full news effect, including its follow-through. Small retracements remain. The price reference follows the realised news change, so mean reversion does not automatically undo the announcement by pulling back toward the opening price.
- The brain excludes opposing stories at the same scope on the same companies, avoids stories whose direction is already blocked by a price limit, and rechecks its choices against the prices at publication. News can still reverse an earlier, different event; the newly published effective set then determines the new reaction.
- `node scripts/news-live-check.cjs` checks actual prices relative to the moment of publication, including all 61 stories in mature markets, overlapping news reversals, both tick speeds and six complete brain-led rounds with three simulated player terminals. This complements the independent-scope and mixed-set checks in `scripts/news-direction-check.cjs`.
- A negative headline means a negative reaction relative to its publication price; a stock can retain a positive overall batch return after that fall. Price retracements and ordinary trading after an effect settles remain part of the game.
