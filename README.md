# Dalaal Street

Dalaal Street is a browser-based market game for synchronized events and classroom activities. Players trade a fictional Indian stock market while a host controls the shared session. It is a learning simulation: prices and company news are generated by the game and are not live market data or investment advice.

## How the game works

- Each participant gets a device session and starts with ₹10,00,000 of simulated cash.
- There are exactly 10 live terminals. Enter the assigned two-digit terminal code at `/play`, or open its direct URL from `/play/01` to `/play/10`; each code maps to that terminal's player session.
- Players can buy shares, sell shares they own, or open short positions. A **Sell all** button beside the order button sells the selected company’s entire owned quantity. It is disabled until shares are owned and leaves other companies and short positions untouched. Prices move as the market simulation, news events, and participant trades update the shared market.
- A host can start, pause, and end a timed batch; set the next batch to 1–15 minutes; adjust market behavior; publish or schedule bulletins; and review participant sessions.
- The market lists 25 companies. The curated news library has 61 fictional bulletins: 12 economy stories, 3 for each of the 8 sectors, and one company story for each of the 25 companies. Hosts can select any mix of categories in Quick Access or News Studio, review the combined stock-by-stock estimate, and expand a headline for its individual effect before publishing.
- A new batch reset restores the market's starting stock prices as well as clearing its terminal sessions and published headlines.
- Host presence uses each device's last-seen heartbeat. A new batch resets participant portfolios without marking previously offline terminals as connected.
- Price movement is driven by 128 simulated traders with 16 distinct trading styles. The baseline volatility is 15% lower than the previous engine setting; hosts can still adjust volatility for an event.
- The audience wall shows the Dalaal-10 level and change, all stock prices and changes, only the latest published news set, and terminal standings without creating a participant account. V4 (`/v4/audience`) is the event display: see **V4 event display** below.
- Audience screens fit the full stock board and latest news into one viewport; the stock grid changes from 7×4 on wide screens to denser responsive layouts on smaller screens. There are no page or news scrollbars on the audience wall.
- Trading screens show a ticker of all stock prices above the market. Only the latest published news set appears near the top of the trading page in a full news panel with numbered stories, scope, major/minor labels, game-relative time and an internal close button. Older sets remain available in the News desk. The live stock ticker runs 15% slower.
- The game includes an isolated practice market with the same three-second countdown, six-minute rounds, trading rules, news flow, settlement, and session report as a hosted game.
- Each player’s round report includes short, activity-based learning notes, badges tied to their choices, and their best and toughest realized closed trades.
- Host notes are stored with the shared game state and are available after sign-in from another device.

## Latest host and display refinements

- Overview shows the live batch clock, start/pause/end controls, terminal status and equity, recent headlines, and shortcuts to frequently used sections. Open it with **Alt + O**.
- Quick Access and News Studio share a faster news desk: category tabs, search, sector and direction filters, a removable selection list, publish/schedule controls, target effects, a reaction timer, and collapsible automation settings. Published picks are cleared automatically.
- Simultaneous news uses the most specific applicable scope for each stock: company news overrides sector news for that company, and sector news overrides economy news within that sector. Stories at the same scope combine.
- News prices no longer jump on publication. The direct price move builds over 35 seconds with small, smooth retracements before reaching the same target. The follow-through eases in instead of front-loading the opening seconds. Its integrated impact is conserved, while the complete effect takes about 15% longer than before (typically 61–94 seconds, within the displayed 58–104 second range). Unrelated background noise is softened only during the direct reaction, so small announcements remain readable while still allowing retracements. Market trading continues alongside news, so displayed effects are estimates, not guaranteed final prices. Pausing freezes news progress; the host timer includes the remaining reaction and follow-through.
- Autonomous news is available at `/auto`. Anyone can start the next shared batch there; that round begins with the news brain enabled. Once started, it selects, reviews, reschedules, and publishes compatible sets of two or three unused stories on its own, without requiring host approval. It studies Dalaal-10 movement, recent price volatility, active terminals, trading activity, and outstanding news reactions, and reviews its choices every five simulated seconds. Repeated reviews keep the original time anchor so publication cannot be postponed indefinitely. The default interval is 75 seconds; the host can set a 50–90 second baseline, and the brain can adapt the next release between 50 and 105 seconds. The host can still monitor and override it.
- The dedicated News brain tab (**Alt + B**) holds the detailed readings, plan, pacing and activity log. Quick Access and News Studio show only a compact start/stop row and a shortcut into that tab. The host can start or stop the brain during an existing batch, review its live market readings, see the upcoming stories and reason, skip and reselect, publish early, replace the next set with two or three host-selected stories, and review the batch activity log. This remains available even when someone else starts the round through `/auto`. Manual news selection remains available when the brain is stopped.
- Audience company names are one pixel larger, and trade input values use theme-aware text and field colors in both light and dark mode.
- The browser tab uses the Dalaal Street brand mark as its favicon.

## Run locally

Requirements: Node.js 20.9 or newer and pnpm.

```sh
pnpm install
cp .env.example .env.local
pnpm dev
```

Open `http://localhost:3000`. Configure the environment values before using the shared market:

- `SUPABASE_URL`: Supabase project URL.
- `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`: Supabase publishable (anonymous) API key.
- `MARKET_SIGNING_SECRET`: a long random secret used by the server to authorize market-state RPC calls.
- `HOST_PASSWORD`: password for `/host`.

`/play` asks for the station's terminal code; `/play/01` through `/play/10` open the assigned numbered player sessions directly. `/v1` is the original look, V2 is an observatory, V3 uses claymorphism, V4 uses expressive flowing forms, V5 is a restrained signal board, V6 is a record store, and V7 uses cut-paper geometry. Every version uses the same market engine, company data, and trade rules. Trading screens adapt their stock columns to screen size and browser zoom; stocks stay on the left, with the selected stock's chart and Buy, Sell, and Short controls together on the right, directly below the chart. Chart markers and hover details show trades from the current terminal's individual session. Audience versions show the Dalaal-10 level and change, the full stock list with each price and change, latest batch headlines, and terminal position summaries.

Starting stock prices use rounded whole-rupee reference closes from NSE India's market page dated 15 October 2026 (15:30 IST); Wipro's close for the same date uses Yahoo Finance historical data. These are opening references for a fictional game and are not live quotes. The initial daily move percentages are preserved to reflect that dated market snapshot.

The practice market uses the same price and curated news simulation as a live round, but has its own state. It starts with a short countdown, runs for six minutes, closes positions at the end, and offers a new practice round. `/test/01` to `/test/10` use their own terminal logins. Legacy autonomous practice sessions are closed safely the first time the updated engine reads them.

During local development, open `http://localhost:3000/ui-preview` to compare the original interface and all six themed versions. Preview market state stays in its own in-memory development store and cannot affect the shared live market. Preview routes return not found in production.

## Supabase setup

Apply `supabase/migrations/20261009000100_market_state.sql` to the project using the Supabase SQL Editor or Supabase CLI. The migration creates the private JSONB market-state table and its access-controlled RPC functions.

Add a row to `private.runtime_secrets` with the same value as `MARKET_SIGNING_SECRET`:

```sql
insert into private.runtime_secrets (id, market_signing_secret)
values (true, 'replace-with-the-same-random-secret')
on conflict (id) do update
set market_signing_secret = excluded.market_signing_secret;
```

Keep the signing secret and host password out of source control. The Supabase publishable key is designed to be public, but this app still keeps it server-side. For Vercel, configure `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, `MARKET_SIGNING_SECRET`, and `HOST_PASSWORD` as environment variables, then redeploy. The host-console login reads `HOST_PASSWORD` server-side; update it in Vercel's environment settings to reset that password. `vercel.json` selects the Mumbai (`bom1`) region.

## Network behavior

The market keeps its one-second update rhythm and price rules unchanged. Unchanged snapshots return a small `304` response, charts send only new candles after their initial history load, inactive screens stop requesting chart candles, and host snapshots omit chart history. All 25 company marks load from small local SVG assets, with a sector glyph fallback if an asset cannot load; screens use system fonts. Disconnected or hidden terminals stop polling; transient request failures back off automatically before retrying.

## Compact brain and situation-aware pacing

- The opening set uses two clear company stories in different sectors, with no more than one major story. The second introduces a sector story alongside a company in another sector. Later sets may use broader combinations.
- Decisions consider session duration, elapsed and remaining time, recent buys/sells/shorts, engaged terminals, positions, unused cash, aggregate portfolio stress and market trends. These are game signals, not a reliable diagnosis of player understanding.
- Quick Access and News Studio keep a compact start/stop row. The dedicated News brain tab keeps its state, Start/Stop, the upcoming stories, release timing and a short decision reason visible. Detailed readings, settings, decision rules, activity and pacing controls are grouped under Advanced. Buttons use their content width so descriptions do not get squeezed into one word per line.
- The brain uses explicit decision rules, calibrated with simulated games. It can choose a calmer two-story set when parents need reading time, a livelier set of fresh opportunities when the market is quiet, or a balanced set when trading is steady. It measures engagement across terminals, recent moves relative to the configured volatility, outstanding effects, held/traded companies and recent topic coverage. It does not use a player’s wealth to favour their results.
- Follow-up stories about the same company or sector are allowed when relevant; repeated headlines, contradictory accounts of the same event and stories reserved by the host are excluded. Normally prior news gets time to settle. A short tail may overlap when the market is quiet, and the brain avoids starting an unfinished new reaction in the closing seconds. The host can still publish a set early.
- The brain may adjust only volatility and bot activity. It observes for 45 seconds, changes at most 5% of the host baseline every 30 seconds, and stays between 75% and 110% of that baseline (within existing setting limits). Disabled bots stay disabled. Every adjustment appears in host activity and expanded readings. Host edits become the new baseline; stopping/resetting/ending restores that baseline. Cash, duration, tick speed, market bias and trading rules are not changed.
- Live price/chart update cadence is unchanged. The scrolling stock-price strip remains 15% slower than its original speed.
- Regression and calibration commands: `node scripts/engine-smoke.cjs`, `node scripts/news-behavior-check.cjs`, `node scripts/brain-scenarios.cjs`, `node scripts/news-direction-check.cjs`, `node scripts/brain-settings-check.cjs`. Scenario tests cover four-, six- and fifteen-minute rounds with spectators, cautious players, active players and a busy market. Direction tests check all 61 stories at three volatility settings: the initial move against publication price, later news contribution against an identically moderated neutral control, and exact isolation of unaffected stocks.

## Shared host logs

- Open **Logs** from the host sidebar or press **Alt + G**. Filter by activity or batch, search for a company/terminal/action, expand event data, load older entries, or download matching records as JSON.
- Records include batch controls, host settings and interventions, terminal joins/reconnections, successful trades and covers, rejected orders, news sets and settling, brain plans and decisions, automatic difficulty adjustments, final settlements, and periodic market checkpoints. Routine price ticks and note contents are excluded. Logging starts with this update; earlier activity cannot be reconstructed.
- The latest 1,500 events persist in the existing shared game store across batches and devices. Reset retains this journal. Player/audience responses exclude logs entirely. Host polling requests journal data only while Logs is open and fetches new entries after the last received event, avoiding repeated downloads of the full history.
- The brain combines current market/participant data with recent logged order difficulties. Repeated failures across active terminals encourage calmer news and gentler market settings; they never cause portfolio changes or changes to trading rules.
- Additional regression: `node scripts/game-logs-check.cjs` checks the actual API handlers, rejected-order persistence without portfolio changes, trade retry deduplication, news and settlement records, privacy, retention and decision signals.

### News direction in brain-led games and updated identity

- The mark is now a compact vector D with a market line, paired with a shared “Dalaal Street” wordmark on terminals, audience screens, host access, terminal entry and the public launcher. The favicon uses the same mark.
- A new news set replaces unfinished effects on the companies it targets. Within that set, company news still takes priority over sector news, and sector news over economy news. Unaffected companies keep their current effects.
- Ordinary noise, bot pressure and old momentum are moderated for the full news effect, including its follow-through. Small retracements remain. The price reference follows the realised news change, so mean reversion does not automatically undo the announcement by pulling back toward the opening price.
- The brain excludes opposing stories at the same scope on the same companies, avoids stories whose direction is already blocked by a price limit, and rechecks its choices against the prices at publication. News can still reverse an earlier, different event; the newly published effective set then determines the new reaction.
- `node scripts/news-live-check.cjs` checks actual prices relative to the moment of publication, including all 61 stories in mature markets, overlapping news reversals, both tick speeds and six complete brain-led rounds with three simulated player terminals. This complements the independent-scope and mixed-set checks in `scripts/news-direction-check.cjs`.
- A negative headline means a negative reaction relative to its publication price; a stock can retain a positive overall batch return after that fall. Price retracements and ordinary trading after an effect settles remain part of the game.


### Trading controls, chart direction and close-of-session results

- The selected stock’s **Sell all** action always uses its complete server-held quantity, regardless of the quantity field. It becomes available immediately after a buy. The redundant “Trade”, “Order panel”, “Order ticket” and “Place a trade” headings have been removed; the chart view controls and trading inputs remain.
- Line and mini-chart colours use the batch opening quote: green above it, red below it, and neutral at it. A drop while above the opening briefly flashes red; a rise while below briefly flashes green. The line then returns to its opening-relative colour. Candle bodies keep their usual per-candle direction colours.
- The brain still selects 2–3 stories. It scores minor/major mixes separately from story count, allowing every combination for both sizes. Opening sets remain simpler; later intensity responds to participation, market movement, unresolved news and time remaining. The plan reason identifies the selected mix.
- Automatic session-close sales and short covers remain clearly recorded in history and now receive the same accounting credit as other trades: total trades, wins/losses, badges and best/worst realised trades. Settlement remains idempotent.
- `node scripts/trading-flow-check.cjs` checks per-stock liquidation, retries, other holdings, paused/empty orders and settlement statistics for individual and timed session endings.
- `node scripts/brain-mix-check.cjs` verifies the brain chooses each of the three two-story and four three-story minor/major mixes under suitable game conditions.

### "That's a wrap" (trading terminals, every version)
The end-of-round screen is set like a printed results page in each version's colours, on one 1080p screen; it scrolls on a short window. There are no boxes, icons, shadows or gradients. It shows:
- **On the left:** "That's a wrap!" and who the player traded like, said in a sentence. The personas are The Watcher, The Bear Tamer, The Sharpshooter, The Speed Trader, The Explorer, The Big Bettor, The Brave and The Steady Hand.
- **On the right:** what ₹10,00,000 became, counting up on a field of the ink colour, with the gain or loss and the place.
- **On one tone step:** you and the market as bars, the best and toughest closed trade, and the round in numbers.
- **Below:** all nine badges as a plain list (the ones still to win say what to do next time), one tip, and the return button.

Press Space (or the button) to return to the ready state. Motion plays once in about 2 seconds and stops with the host's trading-animation switch or under reduced motion.

### V4 trading screens
- Small labels and figures were raised for parents on a 24" monitor: labels ≥ 11px, body text ≥ 13px, prices 15–18px and the header figures (clock, cash, portfolio, P&L) 18px. Layout and other versions are unchanged.

### Round timing and market
- **News brain timing:** every set lands 35–50 game seconds after the last publish (brain or host; the start for the first set; pauses do not count), and at least four sets go out in a 4-minute round. Sets carry two or three stories. Regression: `node scripts/brain-window-check.cjs`.
- **Prices** move in ₹0.05 steps, the tick size of the Indian exchanges; an exact price moves underneath so small moves on cheaper stocks still add up. Regression: `node scripts/price-tick-check.cjs`.
- **Slow connections:** price history is stored packed (about 45% smaller), a poll that changes nothing is answered from a 0.7-second server cache instead of a full database read, and the clocks count down locally between updates. Regression: `node scripts/store-format-check.cjs`.
- Live batches default to **5 minutes** (changeable in host Settings, 1–15 minutes; batches under 4 minutes compress the news brain's clock, with sets at least 15 s apart, so even a 1-minute batch gets two sets and a 2-minute batch three; the terminals' close-out warning starts in the last quarter of a short batch) and open with a **5-second countdown** on the audience stage; practice rounds keep 6 minutes and a 3-second countdown.
- Bots were retuned so news explains most of a stock's move: trend-following, random noise and the bots' amplification of news are all smaller. Without news, a stock now drifts at most about 6% in 5 minutes (previously about 10%).
- When a hosted batch ends, each terminal shows its report for **30 seconds** (with a countdown under the button), then returns to the ready state by itself — at once if the host resets the market or starts the next batch sooner; **Return to ready state** (or the Space bar) still works at once. The chart switches back to **Line** at every new batch and every return to the ready state. Practice rounds are unchanged.
- When the clock shows one minute left, players who still hold shares or shorts see a warning that these will be sold or covered automatically at the closing price; the Portfolio tab says so too. Automatic close-outs still count as trades in the report, and **Toughest closed trade** only ever shows a losing trade.

### Bells and countdown sound
- **Real exchange bells.** The opening and closing bells are rendered like a real electric trading bell: a bank of resonators for the brass gong's vibration modes, struck about 17 times a second with small changes in timing and force, so the rings overlap, beat and ring out naturally, with a crowd of about seventy applauding underneath, all in a hall reverb. The closing bell ends with three gavel knocks. Each second of the 5-second countdown gives a soft tick that rises in pitch towards the opening bell (host → Animations → *Countdown ticks* turns the ticks off). Both bells are rendered once when sound is unlocked, so they start instantly.
- **Rings with the tab in the background.** The audience wall keeps polling and ringing when its tab or window is hidden (a worker timer the browser does not throttle). The **host console** can ring the bells too: the 🔔 button in its header (on by default, remembered per browser) — useful when the board's own speaker is off.
- Trading terminals stay silent.
- Browsers allow sound only after one interaction with the page: tap the board once at setup (a large "Tap to turn on the bells" button appears until you do; a short chime confirms). To remove the tap entirely, start the board's browser with autoplay allowed (Chrome/Edge: `--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required`, or Edge site permissions → Media autoplay → Allow). The same applies to the host console.

### News brain
- **Cadence:** a set every **35–50 seconds**. The brain picks the exact gap from the logs — who acted on the last set's companies and how fast, whether trading is still going, how long the room has been quiet, and rejected orders. It waits only for the previous set's 35-second direct reaction; slower follow-through may continue underneath. Terminals that have never placed an order are ignored when judging reactions.
- **Story choice:** most sets are chosen to move a large part of the market (sector and economy stories are favoured after the opening sets), news is spread across sectors and balanced between good and bad over the round, and the round ends with a closing set.
- **Stable preview:** once a set is shown it stays, unless a story becomes invalid or the brain's intensity (calm / balanced / lively) changes; in the last 20 seconds both the set and its release time are locked, so what the host sees is what gets published.
- **Host controls (News brain tab, Alt + B):** intensity Auto / Calm / Balanced / Lively; **Sooner 15s / Later 15s**; **Keep** or **Swap** a story; **Skip and reselect** avoids the skipped stories and keeps the release time.
- **Clearer tab:** a round timeline, a countdown bar, the expected direct move of each upcoming story, how players responded to the last set, and each factor (in seconds) behind the chosen gap.
- Regression: `node scripts/brain-pacing-check.cjs` and `node scripts/audience-leaderboard-check.cjs`.

### Endurance (20 batches)
- `scripts/endurance-check.cjs` plays 20 live batches back to back with ten terminals trading. The saved game must stay under 200 KB and must not grow from batch to batch; it holds at about 130 KB.
- To keep the free database healthy for a full event, price history is kept to the last 150 candles per stock. Terminal check-ins during a live batch are saved with the next price tick rather than on their own. A screen whose poll is due to save a tick first checks the 100-byte version number, and sends the full game only if no other screen has already saved that tick.
- For long sessions, keep host Settings → price speed at 2 seconds (the default). One second doubles the database work.

### Favour a terminal (News brain)
Host → News brain → *Favour a terminal* makes the brain prefer stories that help one terminal's positions: good news on companies it holds, bad news on companies it has shorted. Stories that would hurt it rank last. Timing and spacing are unchanged. It is useful for helping a struggling first-timer; note that it tilts the competition, including the hall of fame. Players never see it. The choice is recorded in the game log, and choosing *Off* restores fair news. Covered by `scripts/favor-terminal-check.cjs`.

## Order ticket, default quantity and best of the day

- **Buy/Sell always in view.** On short screens (FHD at 125% scaling, ~730 px tall) the order ticket pins to the bottom of the trade column, and the chart and ticket shrink below 820 px of height, so the execute button never needs a scroll. This applies to all seven versions.
- **Price-aware default quantity.** Picking a company pre-fills about ₹50,000 of shares (5% of the ₹10L stake), rounded to a clean lot and capped to what the free cash can buy. Examples: ₹176 → 300, ₹500 → 100, ₹1,195 → 40, ₹3,663 → 14, ₹11,138 → 4, never below 1. The default is set when the company changes, not on every price tick, so it never overwrites a number the player is typing.
- **Best of the day podium.** About 12 s after a batch closes, the final standings on the audience wall turn into "Best of the day": the top 3 traders across all batches (from the hall of fame), each with batch, terminal, gain and return. Nothing extra is read from Supabase.
- **News chime.** When a headline is published during a live batch, the audience wall plays a short, quiet two-note chime. It is WebAudio only: no audio files and no extra Supabase reads. It respects the wall's sound toggle and never plays on page load.
- **Trader feedback.** The "That's a wrap" screen ends with a short form: fun (1–5), learned (1–5) and an optional comment of up to 500 characters. Starting the form pauses the 60 s auto-return. It sends once per terminal per batch and is remembered on that device. Each answer is one small insert into its own table, `private.trader_feedback`, through `submit_trader_feedback`. It never reads or writes the game state row and nothing reads it during play. Practice rounds don't store feedback. To read it: `select * from private.trader_feedback order by created_at;` in the Supabase SQL editor.
- **Animations ignore the OS "reduce motion" setting.** A terminal whose Windows animation effects were off used to show no motion. The host's animation switches are now the only control, so every terminal behaves the same.
- **Feedback sits up top.** The form is directly under the "That's a wrap" headline, so it is visible without scrolling on FHD terminals, including 125% scaling.
- **Host feedback tab (Alt + F).** The host console has a Feedback tab with response count, average fun and learned scores, a 1–5 spread, a batch filter, "only with comments", and a CSV download. It loads only when opened or refreshed, never by polling, through `/api/host/feedback`, which is host sign-in only and backed by `get_trader_feedback` (newest 1,000 live answers).
- **Next.js 16.4.5.** This is a patch upgrade from 16.4.0 that clears the known security advisory. `pnpm audit --prod` reports no known vulnerabilities, and the production build, `tsc` and all 19 checks pass.
- **Feedback name and 60 s report.** The feedback form has an optional name of up to 40 characters, which shows in the host list and the CSV. When a live batch closes on an open screen, the report returns to ready after 60 s. After "Finish session" or a page refresh there is no timer: the report stays until the player presses the button or Space. A refresh shows the last "That's a wrap" again, and its feedback form appears only if that round's form wasn't sent.
- **Host feedback filters.** You can filter by Fun and Learned score: any, 4 and above, 2 and below, an exact score, or not answered. These combine with the batch and comments filters.
- **Better news sting.** The audience-wall news sound is a bright "breaking news" glockenspiel chime: three rising notes landing on a high held note. It lasts about 1.3 s and is quieter than the bells.
- **Feedback layout.** The heading is on top, then fun | learned | name, then comment | send, all on one 44 px control rhythm. It stays fully visible at 1536×730.
